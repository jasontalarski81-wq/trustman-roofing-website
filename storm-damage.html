Types of Storm Damage We See in St. Louis

Hail Damage

Hail is the most common cause of roof damage claims in the St. Louis metro area. Even hailstones as small as one inch in diameter can cause significant damage to asphalt shingles, dislodging protective granules, creating soft spots, and cracking the shingle mat. Larger hail can puncture shingles entirely, dent metal flashing and vents, crack ridge caps, and damage gutters and downspouts.

The tricky thing about hail damage is that it's often difficult to see from the ground. Your roof can look perfectly fine from your driveway while the surface is riddled with impact marks that will shorten its lifespan by years. That's why a professional inspection after any significant hailstorm is critical — damage documented early means a stronger insurance claim and faster restoration.

Wind Damage

High winds — whether from a severe thunderstorm, straight-line wind event, or the occasional tornado warning — can lift, crease, and tear shingles right off your roof. Wind damage often starts at the edges and ridges, where shingles are most vulnerable to uplift. Once a single shingle lifts or blows off, the shingles around it become exposed and are far more likely to fail in the next storm.

Wind damage also affects flashing, ridge vents, and soffits. Loosened flashing creates immediate leak paths, while damaged ridge vents can allow rain to enter your attic directly.

Fallen Trees & Debris

St. Louis is full of mature trees, and severe storms bring down limbs — and sometimes entire trees — onto roofs. Impact from fallen debris can crack or puncture the roof deck, displace large sections of shingles, damage structural rafters, and create immediate water entry points. These situations often require emergency tarping followed by structural assessment and comprehensive repair or replacement.

Ice & Snow Damage

St. Louis winters bring ice storms and freeze-thaw cycles that can form ice dams along roof edges. Ice dams prevent melting snow from draining properly, forcing water to back up under shingles and into the roof structure. The weight of accumulated ice and snow can also stress gutters, fascia, and the roof deck itself.

What to Do After a Storm Damages Your Roof

If you suspect your roof has been damaged by a storm, following the right steps can make the difference between a smooth insurance claim and a frustrating battle. Here's what we recommend:

Step 1: Stay Safe and Document What You Can

Never climb onto a storm-damaged roof yourself. From the ground, take photos and videos of any visible damage — missing shingles, downed tree limbs, dented gutters, debris in the yard. Also photograph any interior damage like water stains, drips, or ceiling damage. Time-stamped photos are valuable evidence for your insurance claim.

Step 2: Don't Make Permanent Repairs Yet

It's understandable to want to fix the problem immediately, but making permanent repairs before your insurance company inspects the damage can jeopardize your claim. You are allowed — and encouraged — to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage (like placing a tarp over an exposed area), but hold off on permanent repairs until after the damage has been properly documented and assessed.

Step 3: Call a Reputable Roofing Contractor

Contact Trustman Roofing for a free storm damage inspection. Our inspectors thoroughly evaluate your roof for all types of storm damage, including the subtle indicators that homeowners and even some adjusters can miss. We document every finding with detailed photos, measurements, and descriptions.

Step 4: File Your Insurance Claim

Contact your homeowner's insurance company to file a claim. You'll receive a claim number and an adjuster will be assigned to inspect your property. Having a professional roofing contractor's documentation in hand strengthens your claim from the start.

Step 5: Adjuster Inspection

When your insurance adjuster visits, we recommend having your Trustman Roofing representative present. We walk the roof with the adjuster, pointing out each area of documented damage to ensure the full scope is captured in their report. This on-site collaboration helps ensure nothing is missed.

St. Louis Storm Season: When to Be Most Vigilant

While storms can hit the St. Louis area any time of year, certain periods carry higher risk:

  • March – June: Peak severe weather season. This is when the strongest thunderstorms, the largest hail, and the most destructive wind events typically occur in the St. Louis metro.
  • July – August: Intense summer storms with heavy rain, lightning, and occasional hail continue through midsummer.
  • September – November: Fall brings a secondary severe weather window with strong cold fronts and associated wind and storm events.
  • December – February: Ice storms and heavy snow loads can damage roofs, gutters, and cause ice dam formation.

After any significant weather event in your area, we recommend a professional inspection — especially if your neighbors are getting their roofs inspected or replaced. Storm damage often affects entire neighborhoods, and the fact that homes around you are being repaired is a strong indicator that your roof may have damage too.
