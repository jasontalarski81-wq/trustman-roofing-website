Why initial insurance estimates often fall short — and how detailed construction documentation and the supplement process can help you get fair compensation.

You filed your insurance claim after a St. Louis hailstorm, the adjuster came out, and a few days later you received the estimate. Then you showed it to your roofing contractor — and learned the insurance company's number doesn't come close to covering the actual cost of replacing your roof.

If this sounds familiar, you're not alone. It's one of the most common frustrations St. Louis homeowners face during the insurance claim process. Initial estimates from insurance companies are frequently lower than the actual cost of a proper roof replacement. But here's the good news: a low initial estimate is not the final word. There's a well-established process for addressing it, and having the right roofing contractor on your side makes all the difference.

Why Are Initial Insurance Estimates Often Too Low?

Before we talk about solutions, it helps to understand why insurance estimates come in low in the first place. There are several common reasons — and in most cases, it's not a matter of bad faith. It's a matter of thoroughness, perspective, and process.

Limited Time on the Roof

Insurance adjusters handle a heavy caseload, especially after major storm events when claims spike across the St. Louis metro area. An adjuster may have 10 or more inspections scheduled in a single day after a widespread hailstorm. That kind of volume means they often spend a limited amount of time on each roof — sometimes as little as 15–20 minutes.

In that timeframe, it's easy to miss damage on certain slopes, overlook compromised flashings, skip a thorough check of pipe boots and ridge caps, or underestimate the extent of damage in valleys and at transitions. Your roofing contractor, by contrast, typically spends 45 minutes to over an hour on a comprehensive inspection because their goal is to identify every area that needs attention.

Missing Necessary Line Items

Insurance estimates are built in software programs like Xactimate, which uses standardized line items for every component of a roofing project. A complete roof replacement involves far more than just shingles. The estimate should account for:

  • Tear-off and disposal of old roofing material
  • Ice-and-water shield at eaves, valleys, and penetrations
  • Synthetic underlayment across the entire roof deck
  • Drip edge at rakes and eaves
  • Pipe boot replacement for plumbing vents
  • Step flashing and counter flashing at walls and chimneys
  • Ridge cap shingles
  • Ridge vent or other ventilation
  • Starter strip shingles
  • Chimney cricket (if applicable)
  • Skylight flashing kits
  • Decking replacement for rotted or damaged plywood
  • Permit fees
  • Steep slope charges for roofs with significant pitch
  • High roof charges for multi-story homes

It's common for initial insurance estimates to omit several of these items — not necessarily intentionally, but because the adjuster either didn't identify the need during their inspection or used default assumptions that don't match your specific roof.

Measurement Discrepancies

Some adjusters use satellite imagery or aerial measurements rather than physically measuring the roof. While these tools have improved significantly, they can still produce measurement errors — particularly on complex roofs with multiple valleys, dormers, hip returns, and varying pitches. Even small measurement differences translate to meaningful cost discrepancies on larger roofs.

Pricing Differences

Xactimate pricing is updated regularly to reflect local market conditions, but there can be lag. Material costs — especially for specialty items, upgraded shingles, or products in high demand after a major storm event — may exceed the prices in the adjuster's software. Labor costs can also vary based on current market demand in the St. Louis area.

What Is a Supplement?

A supplement (also called a "contractor supplement" or "supplemental claim") is a formal document prepared by your roofing contractor that identifies items needed for a proper roof replacement that were not included in the insurance company's original estimate. It is a standard, legitimate, and widely accepted part of the insurance claims process.

A well-prepared supplement includes:

  • An itemized list of additional work needed, using the same Xactimate line items and format the insurance company uses
  • Photographs documenting each area of additional damage or additional work required
  • Written explanations for why each item is necessary — referencing manufacturer specifications, building code requirements, or industry best practices
  • Measurements that correct or supplement the adjuster's original takeoff
  • Pricing that reflects actual local costs for materials and labor

The supplement is not a negotiation tactic — it's a factual construction document that says, "Here is what's actually needed to properly replace this roof, supported by evidence." When prepared by an experienced contractor, supplements are supported with detailed documentation that clearly demonstrates why each line item is warranted.

How the Supplement Process Works

Understanding the supplement process helps set realistic expectations and reduces the stress of a claim that initially looks like it won't cover your roof. Here's how it typically unfolds:

Step 1: Your Contractor Reviews the Insurance Estimate

After receiving your insurance company's initial estimate, share it with your roofing contractor. An experienced contractor will review it line by line, comparing it against their own inspection findings and scope of work. They'll identify:

  • Missing line items (components not included)
  • Incorrect quantities (wrong measurements)
  • Pricing discrepancies (rates below local market costs)
  • Missing code-required items (work mandated by local building codes)

Step 2: Your Contractor Prepares the Supplement

At Trustman Roofing, we prepare detailed construction documentation that identifies every additional item needed for a code-compliant, manufacturer-specification roof replacement. Each item is supported with photographs, measurements, and written justification. This documentation is formatted in a way that insurance companies can process efficiently — because we understand how their systems work.

Step 3: The Supplement Is Submitted to Your Insurance Company

The completed supplement package is submitted to your insurance adjuster or the claims department. This is a straightforward submission of construction documentation — the same way a contractor would submit a detailed scope of work for any construction project.

Step 4: Insurance Review and Possible Re-Inspection

The insurance company reviews the supplement documentation. They may:

  • Approve it outright: If the documentation is clear and well-supported, the insurer may issue an additional payment without further inspection.
  • Request a re-inspection: The insurer may send the same adjuster or a different one back to the property to verify the additional items identified in the supplement.
  • Partially approve: The insurer may agree with some items and dispute others, requiring further documentation or discussion.

Step 5: Re-Inspection (If Requested)

If the insurance company requests a re-inspection, your contractor should be present to walk the adjuster through each supplemented item in person. At Trustman Roofing, we attend every re-inspection and adjuster meeting. Being able to physically show the adjuster each area of concern — and explain why each line item is necessary — significantly increases the likelihood of approval.

Step 6: Revised Estimate and Additional Payment

Once the supplement is resolved, the insurance company issues a revised estimate and an additional payment (called a "supplemental payment") for the approved items. This payment, combined with the original payment, should bring the total claim closer to — or matching — the actual cost of a proper roof replacement.

What Homeowners Can Do to Support Their Claim

While your roofing contractor handles the construction documentation and supplement preparation, there are several things you can do as a homeowner to support the process:

Choose an Experienced Roofing Contractor

The quality of the supplement documentation is directly tied to the experience of the contractor preparing it. A contractor who understands Xactimate, insurance processes, and local building codes will produce documentation that is far more effective than one written by a contractor unfamiliar with the claims process.

Keep All Communication in Writing

When communicating with your insurance company, use email whenever possible. Written records of what was said, promised, or disputed become valuable if any disagreements arise later. If you have a phone conversation, follow up with an email summarizing what was discussed.

Know Your Rights

As a policyholder in Missouri, you have important rights:

  • You have the right to choose your own contractor — your insurance company cannot require you to use a specific company.
  • You have the right to have your contractor present during all inspections.
  • You have the right to request a re-inspection if you disagree with the adjuster's findings.
  • You have the right to file a complaint with the Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance if you believe your claim is being handled unfairly.

Don't Accept a Denial Without Questioning It

If your insurance company denies part or all of your claim, ask for the specific reason in writing. Denials can often be overturned with additional documentation. Common denial reasons — like "pre-existing damage" or "cosmetic damage only" — can sometimes be addressed with evidence from your contractor showing that the damage is storm-related and functional.

Be Patient but Persistent

The supplement process takes time. Insurance companies may take several weeks to review documentation, schedule re-inspections, and issue revised estimates. Stay in communication with both your contractor and your insurance company, and follow up if timelines slip.

When a Supplement Isn't Enough

In rare cases, the supplement process doesn't fully resolve the gap between the insurance estimate and the actual cost. If you've exhausted the supplement process and still believe your claim has been unfairly underpaid, you have additional options:

  • Request an independent appraisal: Most homeowner policies include an "appraisal clause" that allows either party to request an independent appraisal of the damage. Each side hires an appraiser, and if they can't agree, an umpire makes the final determination.
  • File a complaint with the Missouri DOI: The Missouri Department of Commerce and Insurance handles consumer complaints about unfair claims practices.
  • Consult an attorney: If you believe your claim is being handled in bad faith, a property damage attorney can advise you on your legal options.

These steps are rarely necessary when proper documentation is prepared upfront — which is why working with an experienced contractor from the beginning is so important.
