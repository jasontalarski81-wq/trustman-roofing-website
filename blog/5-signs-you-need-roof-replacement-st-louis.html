5 SIGNS YOU NEED AROOF REPLACEMENT IN ST. LOUIS

Know when it's time to stop repairing and start replacing.

SCHEDULE FREE INSPECTION › CALL NOW

Your roof protects everything underneath it — your family, your belongings, your investment. But roofs don't last forever, especially in St. Louis where harsh winters, summer storms, and hail take their toll year after year. Here are five clear signs that it might be time to replace your roof.

SIGN 01

Your Roof Is 20+ Years Old

Most asphalt shingle roofs in the St. Louis area have a lifespan of 20 to 25 years. If your roof is approaching or past that age, it's time to start thinking about a roof replacement — even if it still looks okay from the ground.

St. Louis weather is particularly hard on roofing materials. The combination of freeze-thaw cycles in winter, intense UV exposure in summer, and regular severe storms accelerates wear beyond what you'd see in milder climates. A roof that might last 30 years in a temperate region may only hold up for 18 to 22 years here.

Not sure how old your roof is? Check your home inspection report from when you purchased the house, or look for building permits filed with St. Louis County. We can also estimate your roof's age during a free inspection.

SIGN 02

Shingles Are Curling, Buckling, or Missing

Take a look at your roof from the street. Do you see shingles that are curling at the edges, buckling in the middle, or completely missing? These are signs of advanced wear that patch repairs can't solve long-term.

Curling happens when shingles lose their flexibility due to age and heat exposure. Buckling usually means moisture is trapped in the decking below. And missing shingles after a storm in the St. Louis metro — whether you're in Chesterfield, Ballwin, or South County — leave your roof deck exposed to water intrusion.

  • Curling edges — shingles turning upward at the corners
  • Buckling — raised ridges running along shingle rows
  • Missing shingles — bare patches visible from the ground
  • Cracked shingles — splitting from thermal stress

If the damage is limited to a small area after a single storm event, a repair may be sufficient. But if you're seeing these issues across large sections of your roof, replacement is the smarter investment. Learn more about how storm damage affects your roof's lifespan.

SIGN 03

Granule Loss Is Getting Worse

Check your gutters and downspouts. If you're finding piles of dark, coarse granules — the sandpaper-like coating on asphalt shingles — your roof is losing its protective layer.

Some granule loss is normal on a new roof during the first year or two. But on an older roof, heavy granule loss means the shingles are deteriorating. Without that granule layer, shingles can't protect against UV radiation, and they break down much faster.

In St. Louis, hail is one of the biggest accelerators of granule loss. Even hail that doesn't crack shingles can knock granules loose, shortening your roof's effective lifespan by years. If your home sustained hail damage and you're now seeing granules in the gutters, it's time for a professional assessment.

Quick test: Run your hand across a shingle (safely, from a ladder at the edge). If it feels smooth instead of rough and gritty, the granules are gone. That shingle is no longer doing its job.

SIGN 04

You're Finding Leaks or Water Stains in the Attic

A leak doesn't always mean a dramatic drip through the ceiling. More often, the first signs appear in your attic: dark stains on the underside of the decking, damp insulation, or daylight visible through the roof boards.

If you have attic access, check it after the next heavy rain. Look for:

  • Water stains or discoloration on the wood decking
  • Wet or compressed insulation
  • Mold or mildew growth on rafters
  • Daylight peeking through boards or around vent pipes

Small, isolated leaks around a vent boot or flashing can often be repaired. But if you're seeing multiple leak points or widespread water damage, the decking itself may be compromised. At that point, a full roof replacement — including new decking where needed — is the only real solution.

Homeowners across the St. Louis area, from O'Fallon to Fenton and everywhere in between, deal with this issue more than they realize. Catching it early saves thousands in interior damage repairs.

SIGN 05

Your Energy Bills Are Climbing

A failing roof doesn't just let water in — it lets conditioned air out. If your heating and cooling bills have been steadily increasing without a clear explanation, your roof's ventilation and insulation system may be failing.

In St. Louis, where summer temperatures regularly hit the upper 90s and winter lows can dip below zero, a poorly performing roof forces your HVAC system to work overtime. A new roof with proper ventilation, modern underlayment, and energy-efficient shingles can make a noticeable difference in your monthly bills.

Did you know? A well-ventilated roof replacement can reduce attic temperatures by 20–30°F in summer. That means your AC doesn't have to fight as hard, and your energy bills come down.
