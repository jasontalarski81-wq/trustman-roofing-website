HOW LONG DOES A ROOFREPLACEMENT TAKE IN ST. LOUIS?

Typical timelines for St. Louis roof replacements — from insurance approval through installation day — and what affects how long the project takes.

SCHEDULE FREE INSPECTION › CALL NOW

If you're a St. Louis homeowner facing a roof replacement — whether because of storm damage, an aging roof, or a proactive upgrade — one of the first questions on your mind is probably: how long is this going to take?

The answer depends on several factors, including the size and complexity of your roof, the weather, material availability, and whether insurance is involved. This guide covers the complete timeline from start to finish, with specific considerations for St. Louis homeowners.

Installation Day: How Long Is the Crew Actually on Your Roof?

Let's start with the part most people are thinking about — the actual physical installation. For a standard residential asphalt shingle roof in the St. Louis area, here's what to expect:

Small to Average Homes (Up to 25 Squares)

A "square" in roofing terms equals 100 square feet. A typical St. Louis ranch home, bungalow, or smaller two-story home with a straightforward roof design falls into this category. For these roofs, most professional crews can complete a full tear-off and re-roof in 1 day.

The crew typically arrives between 7:00 and 8:00 AM, completes the tear-off by midday, and has the new roofing system fully installed and cleaned up by late afternoon. Your home is never left exposed overnight.

Larger Homes (25–40 Squares)

Larger homes — including many of the colonial-style, two-story, and multi-wing homes found in neighborhoods throughout West County, South County, and St. Charles — have more roof area and often more complexity. These projects typically take 1.5 to 2 days.

If the job extends to a second day, the crew ensures the roof is fully waterproofed at the end of day one. Underlayment and ice-and-water shield protect your home overnight, and the crew returns the next morning to complete the shingle installation, ridge caps, and final details.

Large or Complex Homes (40+ Squares)

Estate homes, homes with extensive dormers, turrets, multiple roof levels, or complex architectural features may require 2 to 3 days for a complete replacement. These projects demand extra time for intricate flashing work, custom fabrications, and meticulous attention to detail at every transition point.

Specialty Materials

If you're installing a material other than asphalt shingles, the timeline changes:

  • Metal roofing (standing seam): 3–5 days for most residential projects due to the precision required for panel fabrication and installation
  • Tile roofing: 3–7 days depending on size, as individual tiles take more time to install and often require additional structural considerations
  • Flat roofing systems (TPO, EPDM): 1–3 days depending on the area and complexity of the flat sections

Factors That Affect Installation Time

Several variables can push the actual installation timeline shorter or longer than the averages above:

Roof Pitch (Steepness)

Steeper roofs require additional safety equipment, slower movement, and more careful material handling. A roof with a 6/12 pitch (moderate) installs faster than one with a 10/12 or 12/12 pitch (very steep). Many older St. Louis homes — particularly in neighborhoods like Tower Grove, Soulard, and the Central West End — have steep roof pitches that add time to the project.

Number of Layers to Remove

St. Louis building codes allow a maximum of two layers of shingles on a roof. If your home has two existing layers that need to be stripped, the tear-off takes longer and generates more debris. A single-layer tear-off is significantly faster than a double-layer removal.

Decking Condition

Once the old shingles and underlayment are removed, the crew inspects the roof deck — the plywood or OSB sheathing beneath the roofing material. If sections of decking are rotted, water-damaged, or structurally compromised, they must be replaced before new roofing goes on. Minor decking repair (a few sheets) adds an hour or two. Extensive decking replacement can add half a day or more.

Roof Complexity

Not all roofs are created equal. A simple gable roof with two slopes is much faster to install than a complex hip roof with multiple valleys, dormers, skylights, chimney penetrations, and wall transitions. Each of these features requires custom flashing, precise cuts, and additional weatherproofing — all of which take time to do right.

Ventilation Modifications

If your roof replacement includes upgrading or adding ventilation — such as installing a new ridge vent, adding intake vents, or removing old turbine vents — this work adds time to the project but is critical for the long-term performance of your new roof and the health of your attic space.

Crew Size

Reputable roofing companies like Trustman Roofing staff projects appropriately based on roof size and complexity. A typical residential crew consists of 4–8 workers. Larger crews can complete bigger roofs faster without sacrificing quality.

Weather Factors in St. Louis

St. Louis weather is famously unpredictable, and it's one of the biggest variables in the roofing timeline. Here's how different weather conditions affect scheduling:

Rain

Roofing cannot be done in the rain — period. Wet surfaces are dangerous for crews, and moisture trapped under new shingles or underlayment causes problems down the road. If rain is in the forecast, your roofing company will reschedule or postpone. During St. Louis's spring storm season (March–June), rain delays are common and should be expected.

Extreme Heat

St. Louis summers regularly bring temperatures above 95°F with high humidity. Extreme heat affects both crew safety and material performance. Asphalt shingles become very soft in high heat, making them more susceptible to scuffing during installation. Professional crews adjust their work practices — starting earlier, taking more breaks, and being extra careful with material handling — which can extend the timeline slightly on the hottest days.

Wind

High winds make roofing work unsafe, especially on steep roofs or multi-story homes. Most contractors won't work in sustained winds above 25–30 mph. Additionally, shingle manufacturers require certain temperature and wind conditions for proper installation to maintain warranty coverage.

Cold Weather

Asphalt shingles become brittle in cold temperatures and are more difficult to seal properly. Most shingle manufacturers recommend installation when temperatures are above 40°F. Roofing in St. Louis's winter months (December–February) is possible on milder days but requires careful scheduling around cold snaps.

Best Time of Year for Roofing in St. Louis

The ideal conditions for roof replacement in the Greater St. Louis area are typically found in:

  • Late spring (May–June): Warm but not oppressively hot, with longer daylight hours
  • Fall (September–November): Comfortable temperatures, lower humidity, and generally stable weather patterns

That said, roofing can be done year-round in St. Louis as long as weather conditions on the installation day are safe and appropriate. If your roof is damaged, don't wait months for "perfect" weather — get it replaced as soon as conditions allow.

The Full Timeline: From Inspection to Completion

Installation day is just one piece of the puzzle. The overall timeline from your first phone call to standing under a brand-new roof involves several stages. Here's a realistic overview:

Step 1: Free Inspection (Day 1)

When you contact Trustman Roofing, we schedule a free roof inspection at a time that works for you — often within a few days. The inspection itself takes about 45 minutes to an hour.

Step 2: Insurance Claim (If Applicable) — 1 to 4 Weeks

If your replacement is being covered by insurance due to storm damage or hail damage, the insurance claim process adds time. After filing your claim, the insurance company assigns an adjuster and schedules an inspection — which can take anywhere from a few days to several weeks, depending on claim volume in the area.

We meet your adjuster on-site during their inspection to walk them through the damage and ensure everything is documented. The insurance company then prepares their estimate and issues payment, which typically takes an additional 1–2 weeks.

If the initial estimate is too low and a supplement is needed, that process can add another 2–4 weeks for documentation, review, and possible re-inspection.

Step 3: Material Selection and Ordering — 3 to 10 Days

Once financing or insurance is settled, you'll select your roofing materials. Standard architectural shingles like IKO Dynasty are usually available from local suppliers within a few days. Specialty colors, metal roofing panels, or tile may require longer lead times — sometimes 2–4 weeks for custom orders.

Step 4: Scheduling — 1 to 3 Weeks

Your installation date depends on your contractor's current schedule and the weather forecast. During peak storm season (late spring through summer), demand for roofing in St. Louis surges and wait times for scheduling can be longer. Trustman Roofing communicates scheduling clearly and keeps you updated if weather forces any changes.

Step 5: Installation Day — 1 to 3 Days

The big day. As outlined above, most residential roofs are completed in 1–3 days depending on size, complexity, and materials.

Step 6: Final Inspection and Cleanup — Same Day

After installation, our crew performs a thorough quality check, runs magnetic nail sweeps across your yard and driveway, clears all debris, and walks you through the finished project. We make sure your property looks better than when we arrived.

Total Timeline Summary

  • Cash/financed job (no insurance): 2–4 weeks from inspection to completion
  • Insurance claim (straightforward): 3–6 weeks from filing to completion
  • Insurance claim (with supplement): 6–10 weeks from filing to completion

How to Prepare Your Home for Roof Replacement

A little preparation before installation day ensures the project goes smoothly and protects your belongings. Here's a checklist:

Outside Your Home

  • Move vehicles out of the driveway and away from the house. Falling debris can dent cars and damage windshields.
  • Remove or cover outdoor furniture, grills, and planters near the house. Even with protective tarps, dust and small debris can travel.
  • Trim overhanging branches if they interfere with roof access (or ask your contractor if they'll handle this).
  • Clear the perimeter of the house so crews have room to set up ladders, stage materials, and place the dumpster.
  • Mark sprinkler heads near the house with flags so crews don't damage them.
  • Secure gates and let crews know about any pets in the yard.

Inside Your Home

  • Remove items from walls and shelves in upper-floor rooms and the attic. The vibration from tear-off can knock pictures off walls and items off shelves.
  • Cover items in the attic with sheets or tarps. Dust and small debris can sift through during the tear-off process.
  • Expect noise. A roof replacement is loud — hammering, tearing, nail guns, and foot traffic overhead. Plan to be out of the house if possible, or set up in a lower-level room away from the action.
  • Protect your garage. If your garage is directly under the roof being replaced, cover vehicles, workbenches, and stored items inside.
  • Let neighbors know. A quick heads-up about the noise and dumpster is a courteous touch that prevents surprises.

What Happens During Installation

Knowing what to expect during installation day helps reduce stress. Here's a typical play-by-play:

Early Morning: Setup

The crew arrives and sets up ground protection, positions ladders, and has materials delivered and staged. A dumpster is placed in the driveway for debris.

Morning: Tear-Off

Old shingles, underlayment, and damaged components are stripped from the roof. This is the noisiest part of the project. Crews work systematically across the roof, clearing sections down to the deck.

Midday: Deck Inspection and Repair

With the old roofing removed, the crew inspects the entire deck surface. Any rotted, water-damaged, or structurally compromised decking is replaced with new plywood or OSB. This is a critical step — the integrity of your new roof depends on a solid deck beneath it.

Afternoon: New Roof Installation

The new roofing system goes on in layers:

  1. Drip edge at the eaves
  2. Ice-and-water shield at eaves, valleys, and penetrations
  3. Synthetic underlayment across the entire deck
  4. Drip edge at the rakes
  5. Starter strip shingles along the eaves
  6. Field shingles working from bottom to top
  7. Pipe boots, flashing, and penetration weatherproofing
  8. Ridge vent installation
  9. Ridge cap shingles

Late Afternoon: Cleanup and Final Inspection

Crews clean the roof of all debris, run magnetic sweeps across the yard and driveway to collect stray nails, bag all trash, and load the dumpster. A final quality inspection verifies that every component is properly installed. Then we walk you through the finished project.
