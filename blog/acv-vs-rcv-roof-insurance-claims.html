ACV VS RCV FOR ROOFINSURANCE CLAIMS EXPLAINED

Understand how your insurance policy type affects your roof claim payout — and what you can do to get the full replacement cost.

SCHEDULE FREE INSPECTION › CALL NOW

When a St. Louis hailstorm or windstorm damages your roof and you file an insurance claim, one of the most important factors determining your payout is whether you have an Actual Cash Value (ACV) policy or a Replacement Cost Value (RCV) policy. The difference between these two policy types can mean thousands — or even tens of thousands — of dollars in your pocket.

Yet most homeowners don't understand the distinction until they're in the middle of a claim, staring at a check that seems far too small to cover a new roof. This guide breaks down ACV vs. RCV in plain language, explains how depreciation works, and shows you how to make sure your roof insurance claim reflects the true cost of replacing your damaged roof.

What Is Replacement Cost Value (RCV)?

Replacement Cost Value is the amount it costs to replace your damaged roof with a new roof of similar kind and quality — at today's prices. An RCV policy doesn't factor in the age or wear of your existing roof. Whether your roof was 2 years old or 18 years old when the storm hit, an RCV policy covers the full cost to replace it with new materials and professional installation.

How RCV Payments Work

Most RCV policies pay out in two installments:

  1. Initial payment (ACV portion): After the adjuster inspects your roof, the insurance company issues a first payment equal to the replacement cost minus depreciation minus your deductible. This initial check is essentially an ACV payment.
  2. Recoverable depreciation payment: Once the roof replacement is completed and documented, you submit the final invoice to your insurance company. They then release the recoverable depreciation — the remaining amount that brings your total payout up to the full replacement cost (minus your deductible).

RCV Example

Let's say the full replacement cost for your roof is $15,000, your deductible is $1,000, and the insurance company calculates $4,000 in depreciation.

  • Initial payment: $15,000 − $4,000 (depreciation) − $1,000 (deductible) = $10,000
  • After roof is replaced: Insurance releases the $4,000 in recoverable depreciation
  • Total received: $10,000 + $4,000 = $14,000 (full replacement cost minus your deductible)

The key takeaway: with an RCV policy, you ultimately receive enough to pay for a full roof replacement, as long as you actually complete the work and submit the documentation.

What Is Actual Cash Value (ACV)?

Actual Cash Value is the replacement cost minus depreciation — and that's all you get. An ACV policy factors in the age, wear, and remaining useful life of your roof when calculating your payout. The older your roof, the less you receive.

How ACV Payments Work

With an ACV policy, there's only one payment:

  • The insurance company calculates the replacement cost
  • They subtract depreciation based on the roof's age and condition
  • They subtract your deductible
  • You receive a single check for the remaining amount

There is no second payment. There is no recoverable depreciation. What you receive after the adjuster's inspection is the total payout.

ACV Example

Using the same numbers as above — $15,000 replacement cost, $1,000 deductible, $4,000 depreciation:

  • Total payment: $15,000 − $4,000 (depreciation) − $1,000 (deductible) = $10,000
  • No additional payment after replacement

In this scenario, you'd need to come up with an additional $5,000 out of pocket ($4,000 in depreciation plus your $1,000 deductible) to pay for a full roof replacement. On older roofs with higher depreciation, the gap can be even larger.

How Depreciation Is Calculated on Roof Claims

Depreciation is the insurance company's way of accounting for the age and wear on your roof. Understanding how it's calculated helps you anticipate your payout and plan accordingly.

Factors That Affect Depreciation

  • Roof age: This is the primary factor. A 5-year-old roof will have far less depreciation than a 20-year-old roof.
  • Material type and expected lifespan: A standard 3-tab shingle with a 20-year rated life depreciates faster per year than an architectural shingle with a 30-year or lifetime warranty.
  • Condition at time of loss: If your roof was well-maintained, depreciation may be calculated more favorably than a roof with pre-existing issues like moss growth, missing shingles, or poor ventilation.
  • Local labor and material costs: Depreciation is calculated against the current replacement cost in your market — St. Louis pricing, not national averages.

Typical Depreciation Rates

While rates vary by insurer, a common depreciation schedule for asphalt shingles is approximately 3%–5% per year. That means a 10-year-old roof could see 30%–50% depreciation applied, and a 15-year-old roof could see 45%–75%.

For a roof with a replacement cost of $15,000, here's how depreciation changes your payout on an ACV policy:

  • 5-year-old roof (20% depreciation): $15,000 − $3,000 − $1,000 deductible = $11,000
  • 10-year-old roof (40% depreciation): $15,000 − $6,000 − $1,000 = $8,000
  • 15-year-old roof (60% depreciation): $15,000 − $9,000 − $1,000 = $5,000

As you can see, the age of your roof dramatically affects what an ACV policy pays out — making it increasingly difficult to afford a replacement as the roof gets older.

Recoverable vs. Non-Recoverable Depreciation

This is where many homeowners get confused, so let's clarify the terminology:

Recoverable Depreciation

Recoverable depreciation is the portion of depreciation that your insurance company will pay back to you after the roof replacement is completed. This applies to RCV policies. The insurance company withholds it initially but releases it once you provide proof that the work was done — typically a final invoice or completion certificate from your roofing contractor.

Important: You usually have a limited window to complete the work and claim recoverable depreciation. Most policies give you 180 days to one year from the date of loss. If you don't complete the replacement within that window, you may forfeit the recoverable depreciation entirely.

Non-Recoverable Depreciation

Non-recoverable depreciation is depreciation that you will never get back, regardless of whether you replace the roof. This applies to ACV policies. Once the depreciation is subtracted, it's gone. Some RCV policies also have specific line items with non-recoverable depreciation — read your policy carefully or ask your agent.

Which Policy Type Do You Have?

Many homeowners don't know whether they have an ACV or RCV policy until they file a claim. Here's how to find out:

  1. Check your declarations page. This is the summary page of your homeowner's insurance policy. It lists your coverage types and limits. Look for language about "replacement cost" or "actual cash value" under your dwelling coverage.
  2. Call your insurance agent. Ask specifically: "Does my policy pay Replacement Cost Value or Actual Cash Value for roof damage?" Get the answer in writing if possible.
  3. Review endorsements. Some policies start as RCV but include an endorsement that converts roof coverage to ACV after the roof reaches a certain age (often 10–15 years). These "cosmetic damage exclusions" or "roof surface payment schedule" endorsements have become more common in Missouri in recent years.

Knowing your policy type before a storm hits gives you time to make informed decisions — potentially upgrading your coverage if you currently have an ACV policy.

The Growing Trend Toward ACV Policies in Missouri

Over the past several years, insurance companies operating in Missouri have increasingly shifted roof coverage from RCV to ACV, especially for homes with older roofs. Some carriers now automatically apply ACV treatment to roofs over 10 or 15 years old, even on otherwise RCV policies.

This trend is driven by the high frequency of hail claims in the St. Louis area. Insurers are looking for ways to reduce their exposure, and shifting depreciation costs to homeowners is one of their primary tools.

If you receive a renewal notice that changes your roof coverage to ACV, consider:

  • Shopping for a new policy with full RCV coverage
  • Asking your agent about endorsements that restore RCV coverage
  • Understanding exactly what the ACV treatment means for your specific roof age and condition

Tips to Maximize Your Roof Insurance Claim

Regardless of your policy type, these steps will help you get the most from your claim:

1. File Promptly

Don't wait weeks or months after a storm to file your claim. Prompt reporting strengthens your claim and gives you maximum time to complete the work within your policy's depreciation recovery window.

2. Hire a Local, Reputable Contractor

A BBB A+ rated local contractor like Trustman Roofing has the expertise and reputation to produce documentation that insurance companies take seriously. Storm chasers and fly-by-night operators often lack the knowledge to properly support your claim.

3. Be Present During the Adjuster's Inspection

You have the right to be present, and so does your contractor. Make sure someone who understands roofing damage is on-site to point out areas the adjuster might miss.

4. Don't Cash the Check Without Understanding It

Review your insurance company's estimate line by line. Compare it to your contractor's scope of work. If items are missing, your contractor can prepare supplement documentation to address the discrepancies.

5. Complete the Work Within Your Policy Timeline

If you have an RCV policy, make sure the roof replacement is completed within the timeframe specified by your policy so you can recover the depreciation. Don't leave money on the table.
