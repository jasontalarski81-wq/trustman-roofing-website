St. Louis sits squarely in Hail Alley — one of the most hail-prone regions in the country. Every year, severe storms pummel roofs across the metro area, leading to thousands of insurance claims. Here's what you need to know heading into the 2026 hail season.

OVERVIEW

When Is Hail Season in St. Louis?

Hail season in the Greater St. Louis area typically runs from late March through early September, with the highest concentration of damaging storms occurring between April and June. That three-month window accounts for the majority of hail damage claims in St. Louis County, St. Charles County, and Jefferson County.

However, significant hail events can occur outside that window. In recent years, St. Louis has seen damaging hail as early as February and as late as October. The bottom line: if it can storm in St. Louis, it can hail.

DAMAGE

What Hail Does to Your Roof

Not all hail damage is obvious. In fact, some of the most costly roof damage from hail is virtually invisible from the ground. Understanding what hail actually does to roofing materials helps you know why a professional inspection matters.

Asphalt Shingle Damage

Hail impacts knock granules off asphalt shingles, exposing the asphalt mat underneath. This accelerates UV deterioration and can take years off your roof's lifespan — even if the shingles aren't cracked or broken. Larger hail (1 inch or bigger) can fracture the fiberglass mat itself, creating weak points where water eventually penetrates.

Beyond the Shingles

Hail doesn't just hit shingles. A thorough hail damage inspection also checks:

  • Ridge cap — often the first component to show damage
  • Metal flashing around chimneys, walls, and valleys
  • Pipe boots and plumbing vent covers
  • Gutters and downspouts — denting and dimpling
  • Skylights, roof vents, and turbine ventilators
PREPARATION

How to Prepare Your Roof for Hail Season

You can't prevent hail, but you can make sure your roof is in the best possible condition before storm season arrives. A few proactive steps now can save you major headaches later.

  • Schedule a pre-season inspection — identify and fix existing vulnerabilities before storms exploit them
  • Clean your gutters — clogged gutters cause water backup during heavy rain that accompanies hail
  • Trim overhanging branches — high winds break branches that damage roofing; keep them cut back
  • Review your insurance policy — know your deductible, understand ACV vs. RCV, and confirm your coverage is current
  • Document your roof's current condition — take dated photos so you have a "before" baseline if damage occurs

If you already have existing damage: Don't wait for hail season to get it fixed. Pre-existing damage weakens your position on future claims because adjusters may attribute new damage to old wear. Address issues now while you can.

AFTER THE STORM

What to Do After a Hailstorm Hits St. Louis

When the sirens stop and the storm passes, here's the sequence that protects your home and your insurance claim:

  • Stay off the roof — wet, hail-battered surfaces are dangerous. Ground-level assessment only
  • Document everything — photos of all visible damage including gutters, siding, AC units, and vehicles
  • Don't make permanent repairs — temporary fixes are fine, but wait for the adjuster before anything permanent
  • Call a professional roofer — get a free inspection and damage report before filing your claim
  • File your insurance claim promptly — don't wait weeks or months

INSURANCE

Hail Damage Insurance Claims in St. Louis

Hail damage is typically covered under your homeowners insurance policy as a named peril — meaning you're covered as long as your policy is active and the damage is from a verifiable weather event.

Here's what St. Louis homeowners should know about hail claims in 2026:

  • Deductibles are rising — some insurers have moved to percentage-based wind/hail deductibles (1%–2% of home value) instead of flat-dollar amounts
  • ACV vs. RCV matters — if your policy pays Actual Cash Value instead of Replacement Cost Value, you'll receive less. Understand the difference →
  • Time limits apply — report damage promptly; waiting can give insurers grounds to question or deny the claim
  • You choose your contractor — never let your insurance company dictate who repairs your roof

AREAS

Areas Most Affected by Hail in St. Louis

Hail doesn't discriminate, but certain areas in the St. Louis metro tend to see more frequent and severe hail events due to storm track patterns:

  • West County — Chesterfield, Ballwin, Wildwood, and Ellisville often sit in the path of storms moving northeast
  • St. Charles County — O'Fallon, St. Peters, Wentzville, and Lake St. Louis see frequent severe weather
  • South County — Mehlville, Oakville, Arnold, and Festus get hit by storms tracking along the Meramec River valley
  • North County — Florissant, Hazelwood, and Maryland Heights experience their share as well
  • Jefferson County — Hillsboro, Herculaneum, and Imperial are routinely in the path of springtime supercells
