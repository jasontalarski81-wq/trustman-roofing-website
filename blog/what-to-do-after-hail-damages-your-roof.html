WHAT TO DO AFTER HAILDAMAGES YOUR ROOF

A step-by-step guide for St. Louis homeowners.

SCHEDULE FREE INSPECTION › CALL NOW

St. Louis sits in the heart of Hail Alley. When a storm hits, the decisions you make in the next few days can determine whether you get a new roof covered by insurance — or pay for repairs out of pocket.

STEP 01

Stay Safe — Don't Climb on the Roof

After a hailstorm, roof surfaces are wet, slippery, and potentially compromised. Leave the roof inspection to professionals.

Instead, do a safe ground-level walkthrough:

  • Walk your property's perimeter — look for dented gutters, cracked siding, shingle debris on the ground
  • Check vehicles, AC units, and outdoor furniture for dents
  • Look at gutters for granule buildup (dark, coarse sand)
  • Inspect window sills and deck railings for impact marks

Pro tip: If your car or patio furniture shows hail dents, your roof almost certainly took hits too — even if you can't see them from the ground.

STEP 02

Document Everything

Documentation is the backbone of a successful insurance claim. Start collecting evidence immediately.

  • Photos and videos of all visible damage — siding, gutters, windows, debris
  • Save a screenshot of the weather alert for your ZIP code
  • Note the exact date and time of the storm
  • Photograph any hailstones next to a coin for scale
  • Check the attic for water stains or wet spots
STEP 03

Don't Make Permanent Repairs Yet

This is a mistake a lot of homeowners make. Don't fix anything permanently before your insurance company inspects the damage.

You can make temporary fixes to prevent further damage — tarping a leak, for example — but permanent repairs before the adjuster's visit can hurt your claim.

Important: If you repair damage before the insurance adjuster documents it, you may lose the ability to claim it. Protect your home, but wait for the inspection.

STEP 05

File Your Insurance Claim

Contact your insurance company and file a claim. You'll get a claim number and an adjuster will be assigned.

File promptly. Most policies have a deadline for reporting storm damage. Don't wait weeks — call within the first few days.
