HOW TO FILE A ROOFINSURANCE CLAIM IN MISSOURI

A complete step-by-step guide for Missouri homeowners.

SCHEDULE FREE INSPECTION › CALL NOW

Filing a roof insurance claim in Missouri doesn't have to be stressful — but it does need to be done right. Whether your St. Louis home was hit by hail, wind, or a severe storm, this step-by-step guide walks you through the entire process from documentation to final payment.

STEP 01

Document the Storm Damage Immediately

The foundation of every successful roof insurance claim is documentation. Start gathering evidence as soon as the storm passes — the more you have, the stronger your claim.

From the ground, walk your property and photograph everything:

  • Dented gutters, downspouts, and gutter screens
  • Cracked or broken siding and window trim
  • Hail dents on vehicles, AC units, and outdoor furniture
  • Shingle debris in the yard or granules clogging downspouts
  • Any interior water stains on ceilings or walls

Save a screenshot of the National Weather Service storm report for your area. Note the date, approximate time, and type of severe weather (hail size, wind speed if reported). This establishes the weather event that caused the damage — something your insurance company will verify.

Missouri-specific note: Missouri law does not set a hard deadline for filing a claim, but most homeowners insurance policies require you to report damage "promptly." In practice, filing within one year of the storm is standard, but sooner is always better. Waiting months weakens your position.

STEP 02

Get a Professional Roof Inspection

Before you call your insurance company, have a licensed roofing contractor inspect your roof. Why? Because a professional inspection gives you an independent assessment of the damage — and specific documentation to support your claim.

At Trustman Roofing, our inspections are completely free with no obligation. We climb on the roof, check every component — shingles, flashing, ridge cap, vents, pipe boots — and provide a detailed written report with photos.

This report serves two purposes:

  • It tells you whether you have a legitimate claim worth filing
  • It provides professional documentation that supports your case with the adjuster

Many St. Louis homeowners skip this step and go straight to their insurance company. That's a mistake. Without professional documentation, you're relying entirely on the adjuster's assessment — and adjusters work for the insurance company, not for you.

STEP 03

Contact Your Insurance Company and Open a Claim

Call your insurance company's claims line — the number is on your policy declarations page or your insurance card. Here's what you'll need to provide:

  • Your policy number
  • The date of the storm
  • A general description of the damage ("hail and wind damage to the roof, gutters, and siding")
  • Your contact information and availability for an adjuster visit

You'll receive a claim number — save it. An adjuster will be assigned to inspect your property, usually within one to two weeks.

Important: Be factual and straightforward when you describe the damage. Don't exaggerate, but don't minimize it either. Stick to what you observed and let the professional inspection report speak for the details.

STEP 04

Have Your Roofer Meet the Adjuster

This is one of the most critical steps in the roof insurance claim process — and the one most homeowners don't know about. You have the right to have your roofing contractor present during the adjuster's inspection.

At Trustman Roofing, a representative meets the insurance adjuster at your property to:

  • Walk the roof alongside the adjuster and point out all documented damage
  • Ensure nothing is missed or overlooked
  • Speak the adjuster's technical language — Xactimate line items, proper scope of work
  • Provide professional photos and documentation on the spot

This is where having an experienced insurance claims partner makes a real difference. Adjusters are people — they can miss things, especially on a large or complex roof. Your contractor is your advocate during that inspection.

STEP 05

Review the Insurance Estimate Carefully

After the adjuster's visit, you'll receive a scope of loss — an itemized estimate of what the insurance company will pay for. Review this document carefully.

Common issues we see with initial estimates in the St. Louis area:

  • Missing line items — drip edge, ice and water shield, starter strips not included
  • Incorrect measurements — roof area calculated too small
  • No allowance for code upgrades required by current building codes
  • Damaged components not listed — flashing, ridge vent, pipe boots overlooked

If the estimate comes in too low — which happens frequently — your contractor can submit a supplement with additional documentation and line items. This is a normal part of the process. Learn more about what to do when your insurance estimate is too low →

STEP 06

Get Your Roof Replaced

Once the claim is approved and the scope finalized, it's time for the actual roof replacement. Here's how payment typically works in Missouri:

If you have a Replacement Cost Value (RCV) policy, your insurance company pays in two installments. The first check covers the Actual Cash Value (the depreciated amount). After the work is completed, you file for the recoverable depreciation — the remaining balance. Understand ACV vs. RCV →

You're responsible for your deductible — typically $1,000 to $2,500 for most St. Louis homeowners. Trustman Roofing offers 21-month interest-free financing to help cover your deductible or any upgrades you want beyond what insurance covers.

Remember: You have the right to choose your own roofer in Missouri. Your insurance company may suggest contractors, but the decision is yours. Choose a company you trust — one with a BBB A+ rating, local reputation, and real insurance claim experience.
