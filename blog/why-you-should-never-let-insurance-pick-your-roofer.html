WHY YOU SHOULD NEVER LET YOURINSURANCE COMPANY PICK YOUR ROOFER

Your roof. Your choice. Here's why it matters.

After a storm damages your roof, your insurance company might suggest — or even pressure — you to use a "preferred contractor." It sounds convenient. But for St. Louis homeowners, letting your insurance company choose your roofer can cost you in ways you don't expect.

THE TRUTH

Your Insurance Company's Roofer Works for Them — Not You

Let's start with the reality that insurance companies don't talk about: "preferred contractor" programs exist to save the insurance company money. That's the whole point.

Contractors who participate in these programs agree to work at rates dictated by the insurer. In exchange, they get a steady stream of referrals. It's a volume play — do more jobs for less money per job.

What does that mean for you as a homeowner in St. Louis?

  • The contractor has a financial incentive to keep the scope small and the costs low
  • They may not advocate for you when the initial estimate misses items
  • They're less likely to push back on the insurance company or file supplements
  • Their loyalty is to the relationship with the insurer — that's where their work comes from

An independent contractor you hire yourself has one client: you. Their reputation, their reviews, and their future business depend on doing right by you — not by keeping an insurance company happy.

YOUR RIGHTS

Missouri Law: You Have the Right to Choose Your Own Roofer

This is important, so let's be clear: in Missouri, you have the legal right to choose your own roofing contractor. Your insurance company cannot require you to use their preferred vendor. They can suggest one, but the final decision is always yours.

Some insurance adjusters are subtle about it. They might say things like:

  • "We have contractors we work with who can get started right away"
  • "Using our preferred vendor will make the process faster"
  • "We can guarantee the work if you use our contractor"

These statements aren't false, but they're designed to steer you. The "guarantee" they mention is typically no better than what a reputable independent contractor already provides — and often comes with more restrictions.

Know your rights: If an adjuster implies you must use their contractor, politely but firmly state that you'll be choosing your own. This is your legal right in Missouri, and exercising it protects your interests.

QUALITY

Preferred Contractors May Cut Corners to Hit Price Targets

When a contractor agrees to work at insurance-dictated rates, margins get thin. Something has to give. And too often, what gives is materials, labor quality, or scope of work.

Here's what St. Louis homeowners have experienced with insurance-referred contractors:

  • Lower-grade materials — using the cheapest shingle that technically meets the estimate rather than matching existing quality
  • Skipping steps — not replacing damaged ice and water shield, drip edge, or starter strip when they should be
  • Rushed installs — large crews cycling through jobs as fast as possible to maintain volume
  • No follow-up — once the job is done and the insurance company is billed, the contractor moves on to the next referral

A contractor you choose yourself has a different calculation. Their reputation in the St. Louis market — their Google reviews, BBB rating, and word-of-mouth referrals — depends on doing the job right. That accountability changes everything.

SUPPLEMENTS

Independent Roofers Fight for Your Full Claim Amount

One of the biggest advantages of choosing your own roofer is supplementing. Initial insurance estimates frequently miss items or undervalue the scope of work. A contractor who works for you will identify those gaps and submit supplement documentation to get you the full amount you're owed.

Common items that get missed on initial insurance estimates:

  • Drip edge replacement along eaves and rakes
  • Ice and water shield in valleys, eaves, and around penetrations
  • Pipe boot replacement for plumbing vents
  • Ridge vent material and installation
  • Code-required upgrades (ventilation, underlayment) per current local building codes
  • Step flashing at wall-to-roof intersections

An insurance-preferred contractor rarely supplements. Why would they? Filing supplements means pushing back on the company that sends them business. An independent contractor like Trustman Roofing has no such conflict — we submit supplements routinely because getting you your full claim amount is part of the job.

RED FLAGS

Red Flags: What to Watch For When Choosing a Roofer After Storm Damage

Whether your insurance company suggests a contractor or one shows up at your door after a St. Louis hailstorm, watch for these warning signs:

✕ No local address or office

Storm chasers follow severe weather, work cheap and fast, then leave town.

✕ Pressure to sign immediately

Legitimate contractors give you time to decide. Pressure is a red flag.

✕ Offers to "cover your deductible"

This is insurance fraud in Missouri. Any contractor offering this should be avoided.

✕ No BBB listing or online reviews

If you can't verify their reputation, don't give them your roof.

CHOOSE WISELY

What to Look for in a Roofing Contractor You Can Trust

When choosing your own roofer for a storm damage claim in St. Louis, look for these qualities:

  • Local and established — an actual business presence in the St. Louis metro, not a temporary setup
  • BBB A+ rating — verified track record of doing right by customers
  • Insurance claim experience — they know Xactimate, understand supplement processes, and will meet your adjuster
  • Free inspections with no strings — if there's no damage, they tell you
  • Written estimates and clear contracts — no verbal agreements or vague promises
  • Financing options — help covering your deductible legitimately, like 21-month interest-free financing
