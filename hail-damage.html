Hail Damage Is One of the Biggest Threats to St. Louis Roofs

The Greater St. Louis area sits squarely in the heart of the Midwest's hail corridor. Every spring and summer, powerful thunderstorms roll through the region carrying hailstones that range from pea-sized to golf-ball-sized — and sometimes even larger. In fact, St. Louis consistently ranks among the top metropolitan areas in the United States for hail frequency, with the National Weather Service reporting multiple significant hail events in the metro area each year.

What makes hail damage so dangerous for homeowners is that it often goes undetected. Unlike a missing shingle that's obvious from your driveway, hail damage can be subtle — hidden beneath the surface of your roofing material, slowly deteriorating its protective capabilities over weeks and months. By the time a leak appears inside your home, the damage has often spread far beyond the original impact zones.

How to Identify Hail Damage on Your Roof

Hail damage looks different depending on the type of roofing material on your home. Here's what to look for on the most common roofing materials in St. Louis:

Asphalt Shingles

Asphalt shingles are the most common roofing material in the St. Louis area, and they're particularly susceptible to hail damage. Signs of hail damage on asphalt shingles include:

  • Bruising: When hail strikes a shingle, it compresses the underlying fiberglass mat, creating a soft spot or "bruise." Bruised shingles may look intact from a distance, but when you press on the impact area, it feels spongy. This compromised area will deteriorate rapidly and eventually crack or leak.
  • Granule loss: Hail impacts knock loose the protective ceramic granules that cover asphalt shingles. You'll see dark, exposed areas where the granules have been displaced, and you may notice granule buildup in your gutters and downspouts after a storm. Without granule protection, the asphalt layer is exposed to UV radiation and breaks down quickly.
  • Cracking: Large hailstones can crack shingles outright, creating visible splits or fractures in the material. Cracked shingles allow direct water infiltration and must be replaced.
  • Exposed fiberglass mat: In severe cases, hail impacts penetrate through the asphalt layer and expose the white or gray fiberglass mat beneath. This is unmistakable evidence of significant hail damage.

Metal Roofing

While metal roofs are more durable than asphalt, they're not immune to hail. Signs of hail damage on metal roofing include:

  • Dents and dimpling: Hail leaves visible dents in metal panels, particularly in softer metals like aluminum and copper. While small dents may be cosmetic, larger dents can compromise the panel's structural integrity and protective coatings.
  • Scratched or chipped paint: Hail impacts can scratch through the painted or coated surface of metal roofing, exposing the raw metal to moisture and leading to rust and corrosion over time.
  • Damaged seams: On standing seam metal roofs, hail can damage the seam connections, creating potential entry points for water.

Tile Roofing

Clay and concrete tile roofs are common on certain architectural styles in St. Louis. Hail damage to tile includes:

  • Cracks and chips: Hail can crack clay and concrete tiles, especially at the edges and corners. Even hairline cracks allow water to penetrate beneath the tile and reach the underlayment.
  • Broken tiles: Large hailstones can shatter individual tiles completely, requiring replacement of the damaged pieces.
  • Dislodged tiles: The force of hail impact can shift tiles out of their proper position, breaking the overlapping pattern that keeps water out.

Why Hail Damage Isn't Always Visible from the Ground

One of the most common mistakes homeowners make after a hailstorm is looking at their roof from the driveway and assuming everything is fine. The reality is that most hail damage is invisible from ground level. Here's why:

  • Granule loss and bruising don't change the shingle's overall appearance from a distance. You need to be on the roof — or at minimum examining close-up photographs — to see the damage.
  • Damage patterns are inconsistent. Hail rarely damages every shingle uniformly. Impact patterns depend on wind direction, hail size variation, and the angle of each roof slope. One side of your roof may be heavily damaged while another shows minimal impact.
  • Soft metals and accessories are often the first indicators. Before you even look at shingles, check your gutters, downspouts, HVAC units, and metal vents for dents. If these softer metals show hail impact, your shingles almost certainly took hits too.
  • Time reveals what hail hides. Some damage doesn't become visible for weeks or months after the storm, as weakened shingles begin to curl, crack, and deteriorate from exposure to sun and rain.

St. Louis Hail: Frequency and Storm Patterns

St. Louis's geographic location makes it a frequent target for severe thunderstorms. The collision of warm, moist air from the Gulf of Mexico with cooler air masses from the north and west creates powerful storm cells that produce large hail, damaging winds, and tornadoes throughout the spring and summer months.

Historically, the most active months for hail in the St. Louis area are April through July, with peak activity in May and June. However, significant hail events can occur as early as March and as late as September. The metro area — including St. Louis County, St. Charles County, Jefferson County, and the Illinois side — typically experiences 5 to 10 significant hail events per year, with some years seeing substantially more.

Many homeowners don't realize that even "small" hail — stones measuring 1 inch or less in diameter — can cause meaningful damage to asphalt shingles, especially on older roofs or roofs that have been weakened by previous storms. You don't need golf-ball-sized hail to end up with a legitimate insurance claim.
